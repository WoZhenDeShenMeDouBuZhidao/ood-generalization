# Pure CE Grad-L2 Distribution Top-p Sweep

## Question

The 8/15 CE normalized-mean prior did not directly represent the raw CE
Grad-L2 distribution during FITCE training:

- `importance_scale=train_std` multiplied each input gradient by its train
  standard deviation before squaring, so FITCE optimized a
  `Grad-L2 * train_std^2` signal against an unscaled CE Grad-L2 prior.
- `tau=2` made the normalized FITCE signal proportional to
  `sqrt(Grad-L2)`, while the CE score prior itself is proportional to
  `Grad-L2`.

The closest currently supported direct-distribution setting is therefore
`importance_scale=none`, `tau=1`. This sweep tests that pair against the 8/15
CE normalized-mean `--grad-alignment-top-p` sweep.

## Two Remaining Limitations

1. The CE artifact is not a seed-matched exact checkpoint target. Each CE
   repeat is first normalized to sum to one and the normalized vectors are
   then averaged across five repeats, or ten for the three lower-stability
   datasets. One shared aggregate prior is used for all three FITCE seeds.
2. The FITCE gradient objective matches only a normalized relative Grad-L2
   distribution. It does not constrain total Grad-L2 magnitude and therefore
   cannot reproduce the CE parameter state or optimization trajectory even
   if the relative distribution is fit exactly.

These limitations mean this is a test of fitting the available CE relative
prior more literally, not a test that can force FITCE to recover the original
CE solution.

## Controlled Protocol

- Sweep `--grad-alignment-top-p=0.2,0.3,...,1.0` over all 18 real-world
  datasets using the CE Grad-L2 normalized-mean direct-score prior.
- The only training-behavior changes from the 8/15 CE normalized-mean sweep
  are `importance_scale: train_std -> none` and `tau: 2 -> 1`.
- All other settings are fixed: auto categorical encoding, semantic expansion
  `split`, model-space gradient alignment, `reg_scale=0.1`, no warmup,
  class reweighting with scope `all`, gradient term only, train/eval batch
  `256/2048`, learning rate `1e-4`, patience `200`, maximum `5000` epochs,
  and three matched seeds `9803`, `38224`, and `8113`.
- SHAP uses the same sample size `500`. `--record-best-grad-l2` is added only
  as a post-training diagnostic and does not affect training.
- All `162 = 9 p values x 18 datasets` results passed the strict result
  validator. A second complete preflight found `162` existing, `0` pending,
  and `0` invalid results.
- PR is the same mean of 18 per-dataset empirical percentiles against the
  existing 301-sample random-full distributions used on 8/15.

The fixed-checkpoint Grad diagnostic is not numerically identical to the

## Findings

- Removing the TrainStd and temperature mismatches jointly produces a large,
  consistent recovery in selection and random-reference PR relative to the
  8/15 CE-prior sweep. The factorial follow-up below attributes this recovery
  to removing TrainStd, not to setting `tau=1`.
- Better selection does not come with better SHAP alignment: Pure SHAP
  RankDist is worse at all nine p values, by `+0.0622` on average. The best
  Pure SHAP distance is `0.5083` at `p=0.6`, versus the old best `0.4453` at
  the same p.
- The CE target is not fit exactly even under the direct-distribution setting.
  At full features, best-checkpoint Grad KL is `0.1299` and Grad RankDist is
  `0.1844`, not zero. Together with the two limitations above, this explains
  why a CE-derived prior can still produce a model below CE.
- No universal top-p optimum emerges. `p=0.2` is best for macro ID, `p=0.3`
  for macro OOD, `p=0.9` for dataset-mean PR, and `p=1.0` for best-checkpoint
  Grad KL and rank fit.

For future CE-prior diagnosis, `importance_scale=none`, `tau=1` remains the
literal relative-distribution configuration. For CE-prior selection alone,
however, the factorial follow-up favors `importance_scale=none`, `tau=2`.
Neither conclusion replaces the main FITCE defaults for GPT priors, and the
remaining CE gap should not be attributed to top-p alone.

## TrainStd x Tau Factorial Attribution

### Question and Protocol

The joint change above improved selection, but did not initially identify
which transformation caused the recovery. The two missing cells of a 2 x 2
factorial were therefore run:

- only remove TrainStd: `importance_scale=none`, `tau=2`;
- only change temperature: `importance_scale=train_std`, `tau=1`.

The old 8/15 cell is `train_std/tau=2`, and the Pure cell above is
`none/tau=1`. Prior, datasets, seeds, nine top-p values, optimizer, training
budget, reweighting, semantic expansion, SHAP sampling, and all other settings
are identical to the controlled protocol above. All `324 = 2 missing cells x
9 p values x 18 datasets` new results passed validation; the independent
preflight found `324` existing, `0` pending, and `0` invalid results.

### Attribution and Decision

- **Removing TrainStd is the single change that produces the selection
  recovery.** It improves mean ID/OOD under both tau values, and the direct
  old-cell comparison improves ID at `8/9` p values and OOD at `9/9`.
- `tau=1` is not an independent performance improvement. With TrainStd it
  reduces mean ID/OOD by `-0.00577/-0.00657`; without TrainStd the effect is
  still slightly negative at `-0.00126/-0.00120`. The positive interaction
  means its penalty is smaller once TrainStd is removed, not that tau=1 drives
  the recovery.
- The best selection configuration among the four is therefore
  `importance_scale=none`, `tau=2`: its best ID `1.41070` is only `0.00023`
  below reproducible CE `1.41093`, while its best OOD `2.63521` remains
  `0.00725` below CE `2.64246`.
- Both changes worsen SHAP RankDist individually, and the combined setting is
  worst on average. The selection recovery therefore is not explained by
  closer SHAP ranking alignment.
- Keep `none/tau=1` only when the diagnostic question specifically requires
  matching the normalized raw CE Grad-L2 distribution without the square-root
  temperature transform. Use `none/tau=2` as the stronger CE-prior selection
  baseline for subsequent optimization comparisons.
