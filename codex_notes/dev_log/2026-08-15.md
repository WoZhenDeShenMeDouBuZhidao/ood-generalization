# Gradient-Alignment Top-p Sweep

## Protocol

- Sweep `--grad-alignment-top-p` from `0.2` through `1.0` in increments of
  `0.1` over all 18 real-world datasets and eight priors: the six GPT priors
  plus CE Grad-L2 normalized mean and CE Grad-L2 mean rank.
- FITCE uses auto categorical encoding, semantic expansion `split`, model-space
  gradient alignment, `importance_scale=train_std`, `reg_scale=0.1`, `tau=2`,
  class reweighting with scope `all`, the gradient term only, and three matched
  repeats with seeds `9803`, `38224`, and `8113`.
- ID selection is ID balanced accuracy plus ID macro F1. OOD selection is the
  sum of OOD mean/worst balanced accuracy and OOD mean/worst macro F1.
- Dataset Mean PR first computes an empirical percentile separately for each
  dataset against its existing 301-sample random-full (`p=1`) distribution,
  then averages the 18 percentiles. These are reference PR values, not
  p-matched random-ranking PR values.
- Effective-feature SHAP RankDist is the normalized Spearman footrule computed
  only over the semantic features selected by top-p. It is averaged over three
  repeats and then over 18 datasets; lower is better.
- All `1296 = 8 priors x 9 p values x 18 datasets` results passed the strict
  result validator. Recalculation from the individual result files exactly
  matched the sweep summary for ID, OOD, and SHAP RankDist.
- The 144 explicit `p=1.0` results were also compared per dataset against the
  full-ranking results produced before `--grad-alignment-top-p` was
  implemented. All 144 ID/OOD pairs matched exactly; mean and maximum absolute
  deltas were both `0.0` for every prior.

## Findings

- The best sweep ID is GPT `score/score`, `p=0.3`: `1.41147`, numerically
  `+0.00055` over current reproducible CE (`1.41093`). Its OOD selection is
  only `2.63282`, however.
- The best sweep OOD is GPT `seq/rank`, `p=0.5`: `2.63805`, still `-0.00441`
  below current reproducible CE (`2.64246`). The highest OOD Dataset Mean PR
  is GPT `score/rank`, `p=0.9`: `67.5`, also below current CE's `68.6`.
- Adding `p=1.0` does not change any of those overall best configurations.
  Within-prior, full ranking is best for GPT `score_all/rank` OOD
  (`2.63557`) and for both CE mean-rank selection metrics
  (`1.40266 / 2.62844`).
- GPT `rank/rank`, `p=0.6` is the strongest balanced single configuration. It
  is that prior's best ID and OOD result and improves its previous full-ranking
  result from `1.40803 / 2.63487` to `1.40939 / 2.63720`.
- Top-p does not fundamentally repair either CE prior. CE normalized mean at
  `p=0.2` improves slightly over its own full-ranking selection, while every
  CE mean-rank top-p setting is worse than its full-ranking result.
- GPT priors generally have their lowest selected-feature SHAP RankDist at
  `p=0.2`, but selection is usually worse there. Cross-p RankDist comparisons
  must be interpreted cautiously because each p changes both the evaluated
  feature subset and its cardinality; small-k footrule values are also coarse.
- There is no universal best p. The most useful follow-up candidates are GPT
  `score/score` at `p=0.3` for ID, GPT `seq/rank` at `p=0.5` for OOD, and GPT
  `rank/rank` at `p=0.6` as the most balanced configuration.
