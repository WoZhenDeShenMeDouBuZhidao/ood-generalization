# FITCE Training-Range Importance Scaling

## Goal

Test whether a training-range coordinate correction lets FITCE approach the
remove-all-spurious oracle on the heterogeneous-scale `range` dataset while
retaining its advantage on the other synthetic datasets.

The LLM/oracle target distribution is unchanged. For each feature, the loss
uses:

| Setting | Scale applied before gradient L2 |
|---|---|
| `none` | `1` |
| `train_std` | training standard deviation |
| `train_range` | training `max - min` |

With the current `grad_prob_temperature=2`, the optimized distribution is
proportional to `scale * RMS(raw gradient)`. `train_range` therefore matches
the gradient with respect to a conceptual `[0,1]` feature coordinate without
changing the model input or using validation/test statistics.

## Implementation

- Added `train_range` to `--importance-scale`.
- `src/main.py` resolves it from the training tensor only as
  `amax(feature) - amin(feature)`, followed by the existing `1e-6` clamp.
- The resolved scale is used by the existing FITCE gradient and first-layer
  weight paths. No target weights, dataset preprocessing, or trainer behavior
  changed.
- Updated README CLI documentation.
- Verified the resolver on a known tensor: ranges `[8,12]` were recovered
  exactly.
- A small FITCE smoke run completed with `importance_scale=train_range` and
  recorded the resolved feature scales in its result JSON.

## Experiment Settings

- Datasets: `simple`, `range`, `categorical_integer`,
  `categorical_onehot`, and `multi_spurious`.
- FITCE: gradient-only, `REG_SCALE=10`, `loss_lambda=1`,
  `loss_alpha=1`, `grad_prob_temperature=2`, `suppress_bound=0`.
- `LR=1e-4`, `PATIENCE=200`, `REPEAT=3`, `MAX_EPOCHS=5000`.
- Full synthetic data with no train/test cap.
- `SHAP_SAMPLE_SIZE=500`.
- Curves, validation SHAP, and every ID/OOD test SHAP were enabled during the
  run. The test SHAP plots were subsequently deleted; their distance metrics
  remain in `result.json`.
- Reused the matching `none` and remove-spurious results from
  `2026-07-22.md`; only `train_std` and `train_range` were newly run.

- Verified `10/10` new result JSONs have `status=ok`, three repeats, and the
  expected number of resolved feature scales.
- The new runs generated 30 curve plots and 180 SHAP plots. After test-plot
  cleanup, 30 curve plots and 30 validation SHAP plots remain.

All `mean +/- range` cells are mean and 95% Student-t confidence interval
range over three model seeds. Selection is
`Acc OOD Mean + Acc OOD Worst + F1 OOD Mean + F1 OOD Worst`.

## Findings

- Both scale corrections solve most of the heterogeneous-range failure.
  `range` selection rises from `3.1205` with no scaling to `3.6263` with
  `train_std` and `3.6534` with `train_range`; the oracle is `3.6979`.
- `train_range` is the stronger correction on the target dataset. Its oracle
  gap is `0.0445`, compared with `0.0716` for `train_std` and `0.5774` for no
  scaling.
- Both corrections also repair `range` OOD SHAP alignment. Rank/Weight
  distance changes from `0.6111/0.6665` with no scaling to
  `0.2685/0.2924` with `train_std` and `0.2593/0.2857` with `train_range`.
- Macro performance is effectively tied: `train_std=3.7568` and
  `train_range=3.7570`, both close to the remove-spurious oracle `3.7739`.
- `train_range` preserves a large advantage over CE/LAAT on every synthetic
  dataset, but it is not uniformly better than `train_std`. It improves
  `range` and `categorical_integer`, is nearly unchanged on
  `categorical_onehot` and `multi_spurious`, and lowers `simple` by `0.0415`.
- The `simple` decrease comes from one model seed (`3.5917` versus about
  `3.6995` for the other two). This is consistent with observed min/max being
  more sensitive than standard deviation to sample extrema and optimization
  variability.
- `train_range` improves macro OOD SHAP RankDist slightly over `train_std`
  (`0.2456` versus `0.2578`), while `train_std` is slightly better on SHAP
  WeightDist (`0.2353` versus `0.2362`). Neither dominates globally.
- The result supports retaining all three modes. `train_range` is a successful
  scale correction and the best current choice for the raw `range` stress
  test, but the five-dataset macro does not justify replacing `train_std`
  globally without a robustness treatment for extrema.
