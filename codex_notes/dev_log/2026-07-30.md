`acshealthinsurance` are stable or caused by the earlier `REPEAT=3` variance.

## Settings

- Dataset: `acs/acshealthinsurance`.
- Methods: MLP CE and FITCE.
- Reweighting: explicit `off` versus `on`.
- `REPEAT=5`; seeds: `9803`, `38224`, `8113`, `4854`, and `98825`.

# FITCE Zero-Regularization Equivalence and Diagnostics

## Settings

- Datasets: `acspubliccoverage` and `acshealthinsurance`.
- `REPEAT=5`, `PATIENCE=200`, `LR=1e-4`, and dataset-level `reweighting=auto`.
- FITCE: GPT-5.4 semantic `rank/rank`, gradient term only,
  `lambda=1`, `alpha=1`, `tau=2`, no importance scaling, and no warmup.
- CE generated curve and validation SHAP plots. FITCE with `REG_SCALE=0`
  generated performance only. After equivalence was confirmed, FITCE with
  `REG_SCALE=0.1` generated curve and validation SHAP plots.

## Diagnostic Isolation Fix

Curve diagnostics previously performed their extra gradient forward while the
MLP remained in training mode. This updated BatchNorm running statistics a
second time per batch and allowed plotting to change model performance.
`diagnostic_gradient_terms` now evaluates the diagnostic forward in eval mode
and restores the prior model mode. A direct check confirms that training mode,
BatchNorm running mean, and BatchNorm running variance are preserved.
Diagnostics are also collected only for the first three repeats that produce
plots.

## 2026-07-27 Versus Current Macro Gap

The 2026-07-27 comparison reported MLP CE `2.5623` and FITCE `2.5431`, a
`0.0192` gap. The current frozen-policy values are MLP CE `2.5716` and FITCE
`2.5172`, a `0.0544` gap.

These experiments differ in two relevant ways:

1. seven dataset `reweighting=auto` defaults changed from `off` to `on`;
2. FITCE changed from `importance_scale=train_std` to
   `importance_scale=none`.

No dataset changed from reweighting `on` to `off`. The 2026-07-29 paired
on/off screen isolates the effect of each reweighting-map change under the
current no-scaling FITCE setting:

| **Macro over 18 datasets** | -- | -- | **+0.0093** | **+0.0549** | **+0.0456** |

`Relative Gap Change = FITCE Change - CE Change`; positive values mean the
map change helps FITCE relative to CE. Therefore, the new reweighting map
narrows rather than widens the FITCE deficit. The largest relative recoveries
come from `acstraveltime`, `nhanes_lead`, and `acspubliccoverage`.

Using the on/off screen to reconstruct the old auto map under
`importance_scale=none` gives FITCE approximately `2.4623`. The macro change
can therefore be decomposed as follows; small discrepancies are due to
four-decimal values in the dev-log tables:

| Step | MLP CE | FITCE | FITCE Minus CE |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2026-07-27: old map + `train_std` | 2.5623 | 2.5431 | -0.0192 |
| Old map + `none` | 2.5623 | 2.4623 | -0.1000 |
| Current map + `none` | 2.5716 | 2.5172 | -0.0544 |

Removing `train_std` costs FITCE approximately `0.0808` under the old map.
The current reweighting map then recovers approximately `0.0549` for FITCE
while adding `0.0093` to CE. Thus the observed gap widening is primarily due
to `train_std -> none`; the reweighting changes offset about `0.0456` of that
relative loss.

### Selection-Deficit Correlation

Define `deficit = CE Selection - FITCE Selection = -Selection Delta`; larger
deficit means worse FITCE performance. A positive correlation would support
the hypothesis that a larger distance accompanies a larger FITCE deficit.
These are exploratory dataset-level correlations over 18 means and do not
model the uncertainty of each Selection Delta.

| Distance vs. Deficit | Pearson | p-value | Spearman | p-value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Grad RankDist | -0.343 | 0.164 | +0.110 | 0.663 |
| Grad Raw WeightDist | -0.292 | 0.240 | -0.123 | 0.627 |
| SHAP RankDist | +0.027 | 0.917 | -0.280 | 0.261 |
| SHAP WeightDist | **+0.624** | **0.006** | +0.335 | 0.174 |

Only SHAP WeightDist has a significant full-sample Pearson relationship.
However, its weaker, non-significant Spearman relationship shows that this is
not a consistent monotonic ordering. Excluding the two largest deficits
(`acspubliccoverage` and `acshealthinsurance`) reduces the SHAP WeightDist
relationship to Pearson `r=+0.160` (`p=0.554`) and Spearman `rho=+0.071`
(`p=0.795`).

|---|---:|---:|
| Grad RankDist | 0.4326 | 0.4544 |
| Grad Raw WeightDist | 0.4006 | 0.4647 |
| SHAP RankDist | 0.4063 | 0.5086 |
| SHAP WeightDist | **0.6821** | **0.4936** |

### Interpretation

- Poor selection does not generally imply worse gradient alignment.
  `acspubliccoverage`, the largest performance failure, has the smallest Grad
  RankDist and second-smallest Grad Raw WeightDist among all 18 datasets.
  FITCE can therefore fit its local gradient target while substantially
  reducing predictive performance.
- SHAP RankDist also does not explain selection. Its correlation is effectively
  zero by Pearson and points in the opposite direction by Spearman.
- The only useful signal is SHAP WeightDist: the worst-performing datasets
  often have more mismatched SHAP magnitude distributions. This supports a
  possible failure to transfer local gradient control into global feature-use
  magnitudes, but the association is concentrated in the largest failures and
  is not sufficient by itself.
- Counterexamples remain: `acsmobility` has the second-largest SHAP WeightDist
  (`0.9045`) but nearly neutral Selection Delta (`-0.0084`), while
  `nhanes_lead` has a relatively large SHAP WeightDist (`0.5963`) and the best
  Selection Delta (`+0.0849`).
- Overall, distance is not a general proxy for performance. The results are
  more consistent with a combination of prior quality, local-to-global
  attribution transfer, and optimization interaction than with a single
  alignment-failure explanation.

## Protocol

- Datasets: all 18 real-world datasets; synthetic datasets are excluded.
- Methods: MLP CE, FITCE, and LAAT.
- Reweighting was explicitly compared with `off` and `on`.
- `REPEAT=3`, `PATIENCE=200`, `MAX_EPOCHS=5000`, and `LR=1e-4`.
- FITCE used semantic GPT-5.4 `rank/rank`, gradient term only,
  `REG_SCALE=0.1`, `tau=2`, no importance scaling, and no warmup.

  `REG_SCALE=0.1`.

### Frozen Policy

All real-world datasets use class reweighting for CE, FITCE, LAAT, and other
compatible methods. This is a shared protocol choice based only on ID
Balanced Accuracy + Macro-F1; it is not selected separately per method or
using OOD test results. Synthetic datasets remain unweighted.
This decision supersedes the earlier dataset-specific policy discussed above.
