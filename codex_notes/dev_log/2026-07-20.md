### Diagnosis

- FITCE successfully fits the gradient ranking on most datasets: 13/18 have
  Grad RankDist at most `0.1`, and 16/18 are at most `0.2`.
- The two clearest optimization failures are `acshealthinsurance` and
  `acsincome`. Their best epochs are 1 and 3, so validation selection chooses a
  model before gradient alignment develops. This indicates an immediate
  conflict between the regularized training path and validation performance,
  rather than insufficient patience.
- Successful gradient fitting does not rescue performance. Among the 16
  datasets with Grad RankDist at most `0.2`, FITCE still trails MLP CE by
  `0.1066` selection points on average and wins only 4/16 datasets.
- SHAP remains much farther from the prior than the training gradient:
  Grad RankDist is `0.0995`, while SHAP RankDist is `0.4746`. The current loss

  that ordering to global SHAP feature use.
- The evidence rejects "FITCE usually fails to fit its gradient prior" as the
  primary explanation. It instead points to two downstream issues that this
  experiment cannot fully separate: the real-world LLM prior may not describe
  the predictive features needed under each shift, and local gradient matching
  may be an insufficient proxy for global feature reliance.

### Interpretation

- Train-std clearly addresses SHAP concentration/alignment in many cases. SHAP
  WeightDist improves on 16/18 datasets and SHAP RankDist improves on 12/18.
- The clearest recoveries are `acsincome` (`+1.4905` selection, SHAP WeightDist
  `0.7654 -> 0.1637`) and `acsincomepovertyratio` (`+0.3275`,
  `0.7340 -> 0.2142`). Their plots change from one-feature-dominated SHAP to a
  substantially broader feature distribution.
- This fix is not a general performance improvement. Macro selection is nearly
  unchanged because 7 dataset gains are offset by 11 losses. Accuracy drops
  while F1 improves, so train-std also changes the operating behavior rather
  than simply stabilizing attribution.
- Several poor datasets remain poor despite less extreme SHAP, especially
  `acshealthinsurance`, `acspubliccoverage`, and `whyshift/accident`. This shows
  that SHAP concentration is a real failure mode but not the only cause of the
  FITCE performance gap.
- `importance_scale=train_std` should therefore not replace no scaling as a
  global default based on this run. It fixes specific datasets strongly but
  requires either dataset-aware selection or a different scale formulation to
  avoid the paired performance regressions.
